# Forgot to Renew Your ESTA and Only Found Out at the Airport? Here’s What to Do

This is one of those things you’re sure won’t happen to you.

You arrive at the airport, your bags are packed, the hotel is waiting, maybe you already have your first meeting in New York scheduled, and then at the check-in counter you hear the sentence nobody wants to hear:

**“Your ESTA is not valid.”**

It happened to me.

So first of all: it’s stressful. Very stressful.

But before panicking, it helps to understand what ESTA actually is, why the airline can’t simply “let it slide,” and what you can still do even if you only discover the problem at the airport.

## So, What Exactly Is ESTA?

ESTA stands for **Electronic System for Travel Authorization**.

It is an electronic travel authorization that allows eligible citizens of countries participating in the U.S. Visa Waiver Program to travel to the United States for tourism or business for stays of up to 90 days without applying for a regular visitor visa.

Israel joined the Visa Waiver Program in 2023, and eligible Israeli citizens can apply for ESTA using a qualifying biometric passport.

It is important to understand that **ESTA is not a visa, and it does not guarantee entry into the United States**. It allows you to travel to the U.S. and request admission. The final decision is made by U.S. border authorities when you arrive.

## How Do You Apply for ESTA?

The application is completed online.

My strongest recommendation is to apply **only through the official U.S. government ESTA website**:

**[Official ESTA Application Website](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/)**

There are many third-party websites that look official but simply charge additional service fees for submitting the same application.

You will generally need your passport, email address, contact information, emergency contact details, a selfie or passport image, and information about your trip and background.

An approved ESTA is generally valid for **two years, or until your passport expires, whichever comes first**, and can usually be used for multiple trips.

And this is exactly where it’s easy to make a mistake.

You remember applying for ESTA before. You travelled to the U.S. previously without any problem. So naturally, you assume it is still valid.

Sometimes it isn’t.

## What Happens If You Get to the Airport and Your ESTA Is No Longer Valid?

### 1\. The Airline Cannot Simply Put You on the Flight

If you are travelling to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program, you need a **valid, approved ESTA before boarding your flight**.

So if the airline’s system shows that you do not have valid authorization, the check-in staff generally cannot simply ignore it and allow you to board.

Even if your passport is valid.

Even if your ticket is fully paid.

And even if your flight is leaving in an hour.

### 2\. Forgot to Renew It? You Can Still Apply from the Airport

Here is the good news.

If you only discover at the airport that your ESTA has expired, **you can still immediately submit a new application from your phone** through the official website:

**[https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov/)**

There is no rule preventing you from applying on the day of travel.

However, U.S. authorities recommend applying at least **72 hours before departure**, and there is a reason for that.

So if you forgot, it is absolutely worth applying immediately.

Just understand that there is no guarantee that the approval will arrive before your boarding gate closes.

### 3\. Sometimes ESTA Is Approved Immediately, and Sometimes It Shows “Authorization Pending”

From the traveller’s perspective, there are usually two possible experiences.

Sometimes you submit the application and receive:

**Authorization Approved**

very quickly.

At that point, you can finally relax.

In other cases, the result is:

**Authorization Pending**

which means that the application is still being processed and additional review is required.

People often refer to this as a “manual review,” but technically it is better to call it **Authorization Pending**. The U.S. authorities do not state that every pending application is necessarily reviewed manually by a person.

In my personal case, the additional review was completed relatively quickly.

So if you are standing at the airport and receive a pending status, I would not immediately give up.

That said, the official processing window can be **up to 72 hours**.

So even though some pending applications may be resolved within an hour or two, you should not treat that as guaranteed.

## What If Your Flight Has Already Left?

At this point, the situation moves from ESTA to the airline.

If your ESTA was not approved in time and you missed your flight, that does not necessarily mean your entire trip is over or that you immediately need to buy a completely new ticket.

**Speak to the airline representatives before doing anything else.**

Depending on the airline, your ticket conditions, and seat availability, they may be able to move you to another flight, sometimes even the following day.

This may depend on:

-   Seat availability on later flights
    
-   The fare rules of your original ticket
    
-   Any fare difference
    
-   Change fees, if applicable
    
-   The airline’s own policy
    
-   The specific circumstances of your case
    

The check-in representatives can usually explain your options.

You may then need to go to the airline’s ticketing or sales desk so they can check alternative flights and process the change.

My advice is simple:

**Do not immediately leave the airport, and do not rush to buy a brand-new ticket online before asking the airline what they can do for you.**

The difference in cost can be significant.

## What Did I Learn from This?

There is a very simple reason I am writing this post:

**It happened to me.**

ESTA is one of those small travel details that is incredibly easy to forget.

You apply once, it stays valid for a long period, there is no physical document sitting in your wallet reminding you about it, and most of the time you have absolutely no reason to think about it.

Until you are standing at the check-in counter.

It is not a pleasant experience.

But if you discover the problem at the last minute, **it does not necessarily mean everything is lost**.

Open the official ESTA website immediately.

Submit a new application.

Stay in contact with the airline staff.

Check every available option before giving up on the trip.

And next time?

I already know exactly what I’m doing.

**Checking my ESTA status a few days before the flight.**

Because some things are much better discovered at home than at the airport check-in counter.
